About the Lab

The Hou Lab develops data-driven computational methods — machine learning, deep learning, and computational optimization — to address fundamental problems in the biological sciences.

Our core interests include protein and RNA structure prediction, molecular interactions (protein–protein complexes, protein–RNA contacts), and quality assessment of predicted molecular structures. We also participate in collaborative projects in healthcare analytics, privacy-preserving machine learning, and omics data analysis, and we are actively exploring how AI agents and large language models can automate structural biology workflows.

The lab is led by Dr. Jie Hou, a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Department of Computational Mathematics, Science and Engineering (CMSE) at Michigan State University. Before joining MSU in January 2026, Dr. Hou was an Associate Professor (with tenure) in the Department of Computer Science at Saint Louis University, where the lab was founded in 2019.

Research Areas

Protein & RNA Structure Prediction and Quality Assessment

Deep learning and data-assisted (SAXS, cryo-EM) modeling of biomolecular 3D structures, AI-ready RNA motif data, and graph neural networks that estimate the accuracy of predicted proteins and complexes without a reference.

Funded projects NIH R15 GM155891 NIH R15 GM085699

Omics & Health Data Analytics

Machine learning for gene regulation and genomic imbalance, single-cell and proteomics data analysis, and pharmacogenetic warfarin dosing deployed through the electronic health record.

Funded projects NIH R01 HL173734

AI Agents, Research Security & Education

LLM agents for automated RNA motif analysis, security risks to scientific integrity in AI-driven biology, privacy-preserving learning, and AI-ready education from urban planning to coding.

Funded projects NSF EAGER 2628291 NSF CC* 2430236 MSU AI Ready Spartans

New NSF EAGER award on research security in AI-driven biology

Dr. Hou received an NSF award, Collaborative Research: EAGER: Unveiling Security Risks to Scientific Integrity in AI-Driven Biological Discovery, to study how unverified external datasets and models can compromise biological AI workflows. Award 2628291.
